Mélarrne is one of the great trade cities of the [[Southern Reach]], situated on the eastern edge of the [[Kalakalan Coast]]. It is a large city-state built upon the [[Arjos Wetlands]]. Mélarrne is one of the most long-standing city-states in the region, having been founded early in the Empire's [[Golden Age of the Empire|Golden Age]]. It remained independent for much of its life before swearing fealty to the Empire during the Age of Despair. Though a part of the fold, the Empire's control has remained loose. As such, Mélarrne remains a cosmopolitan centre of trade, culture, and wealth.
### Notable People and Groups
The most prominent groups in Mélarrne are its ruling merchant families, and the dozens of minor families which propagate from them. Many of these were present at the foundation of the city-state and have remained in prominence by way of commerce and the city's strict constitution. Of these families, there are seven:
- **Therizon** – The Therizon family is one of the more recent additions to Mélarrne, having only arrived within the last three centuries. After many years of waiting in the realm of lower nobility, they took the place of the fallen Senquisse family whose adherence to the faith of Viterna during Mélarrne's assimilation cost them their reputation and holdings. They were allied closely with the Delameur family, but were disgraced in a scandal involving both houses. The family's heads, Orrivel and Sorathe Therizon, currently reside in Azkhar Prison, while their daughter Belisha has been transported to a prison in the Heartlands.
- **Delameur** – One of the oldest and most mysterious families of Mélarrne, the Delameur family rose to prominence on the back of their famed perfumes and their propensity for political manipulation. Until recently, Marchand Delameur IV was considered the most eligible suitor in Mélarrne, but upon the discovery of his status as a vampire, the house was completely dismantled and its operations divided between Mélarrne's other great houses. Marchand's whereabouts are currently unknown, but he is assumed alive.
- **Cavareth** – The Cavareth family made its name upon the morbid practice of caring for Mélarrne's dead. When the soil of the Arjos Wetlands proved too weak for interment, the Cavareth family devised the current schema of publicly displaying the cleaned skeletons of Mélarrne's deceased at public parks. Though initially wary, Mélarrne's public eventually embraced the unique method of burial to the point where the practice became a mark of the city's pride. The current heads of of the family, Andular and Seredin Cavareth, now greatly assist the Mirian Embassy in the investigation of the Delameur Scandal by properly interring Delameur's victims into a new public memorial.
- **Orvaille** – The Orvaille family gained their standing not through wealth, but through their expeditions into the Fellreach and the miraculous powers of insight they gained therein. In the late Golden Age, Naraleth Orvaille made his name as a hunter of gorgons within the depths of the hostile territory. He took their eyes as prizes, and through arcane means, transplanted them into the bodies of his descendants. These eyes have been passed down ever since, as they have yet to fester due to age and imbue the user with divinatory insights at the cost of their physical appearance. The family thus adopted the use of masks to conceal their grotesque appearances. 
- **Saskaar** – Saskaar is the house that faith built. They were among the foremost settlers of the region, and spent much of their early years in Mélarrne maintaining its various shrines. In their search for more prestigious offerings to the various willspawn worshiped in the city-state. Their expeditions into the Fellreach merited the discovery of Ilsyra inks, derived from the sap of a tree of the same name. These inks are highly conductive, and continue to be used in sacred and arcane texts today. Following Mélarrne's assimilation into the empire, the Saskaar house fought tirelessly to secure religious freedoms, exchanging the funding of Mirian temples for those privileges. They currently maintain three major shrines—to Nali, Dwir, and Khata—along with several Mirian temples. 
- **Mirelant** – A family of masons and architects, the Mirelant family is responsible for taming the stubborn Arjos Wetlands into the vibrant streets that Mélarrne currently enjoys. With their unique grasp on earth- and stonework, the wetlands were built up with retaining walls that now form the intricate network of canals and bridges that make transportation in the Mélarni streets possible. In their architectural efforts, the Mirelant family chose to work with the surrounding scenery instead of against it, leading to the sharp and imposing spires and rooftops that decorate the Mélarni skyline. Similarly, their foresight and knowledge of geological structure allowed for the piping of the natural gasses that provided Mélarrne with the lanternlight that makes the Mélarni nights so distinctive. 
- **Zharelle** – Though they came to the Mélarrne as smugglers, the Zharelle family is solely responsible for the design of Mélarrne's naval prominence. Their ships were designed with such geometry which kept them as light as they were hardy. One could not trade along the coast of Kalakalan without witnessing the beautifully-drawn sails of a Zharelle ship. Their history as criminals initially barred them from both the Grand Council and from Mélarrne's elections, but following their ships' efficacy against Olum the Giant in the Age of Despair, their name was lifted into legitimacy. They have since served twice as the city-state's Héldrion, and in doing granted Mélarrne great fortune in those decades. 
Aside from the great houses, the willspawn Miria has sent missionaries and ordained soldiers to serve and monitor the community of Mélarrne. They have until recently maintained a loose presence in the city-state's affairs, but with the light shone on the Delameur scandal, they took a more active role in Mélarrne's politics. The Mirian Embassy now controls the Delameur household, and is currently investigating the depths of the corruption there.
### Geography
Mélarrne is nestled in the Arjos Wetlands, a delta at the end of the River Shiozsho guided by the tall roots of jungle trees that braid the waterways into a labyrinth of canals and silty islets. The city proper rests between two sheer limestone cliffs—the Eizsho and Anzsho—which act as the city's natural walls. These cliffs protect the delta from storms, and guide incoming ships through the glittering [[Frisian Bay]]. The wetlands are both bounty and bane, providing fish, spices, and exotic woods as much as they harbour predators and pestilence.

To the east is the Fellreach, an expanse of choking swamps and shaded jungles rumoured to harbour the most fearsome of Astrana's beasts. Exotic riches can be found in the Fellreach, the most prized of which are Ilsyra inks, dragon scales, and gorgon's eyes. These are guarded by horrors great and small, and pose a challenge to any would-be explorers and a threat to an otherwise peacefully-minded society.

Together, these geographic boundaries make Mélarrne a crossroads of exotic trade and a citadel of precarious survival—half-guarded by cliffs, half-threatened by swamp and shadow.
### History
Mélarrne began its life not as a city, but as a haven for refugees from the Empire's Golden Age. When the Watch searched the land for heretics, dissidents, and impure subjects, groups of persecuted fugitives fled to the Arjos Wetlands. Between the cliffs and islets, they built stilted homes and shrines to their forbidden gods. Swamp and sun sheltered them from the empire, while bay and cliff sheltered them from the massive storms that harry the Kalakalan Coast.

Over the ages, the camp grew into a thriving canal town. Its location on river and sea routes, along with its near proximity to the Fellreach made it a natural candidate for exotic trade. To manage wealth and power, the guilds and aristocrats established a constitution with strict electoral rites that kept power in the hands of its wealthiest merchants. The rites enshrined in the constitution relied as much on spectacle and chance as they did the rationality of the merchants proctoring the elections. These elements, along with the rites' homage to tradition, ensured the city-state's stability over centuries of its vivacious growth.

During the Age of Despair, Mélarrne faced the gravest of its perils. It had been no stranger to jealous siege before, but the opponent they faced in Olum the Giant was far and beyond the efforts of meagre privateers. Olum saw the city as a jewel within his realm, a fixture denied to his kin. He united the marsh-giant tribes utilising reason and charisma uncharacteristic of other Fellreach denizens, and laid siege upon Mélarrne with tides of beasts and curses. The republic fought desperate wars in the canals and shallows which often hid the marsh-giants' presence, but only survived with divine intervention and infantry support from the Mirian Heartlands.

Following Olum's defeat, the Empire offered protection in exchange for a loose assimilation into its fold. Confronted by the reality of the Fellreach's threats, Mélarrne entered into negotiations with Empire officials. The hearings were lengthy, with much consideration being given to religious, economic, and governmental freedom. Mélarrne leaned on its wealth, offering funding for the Empire's public works and temples to Miria built within its walls. In turn, the Empire's soldiers and priests joined the city's defences, turning back Olum's sieges until his death in the early years of The Divine Resurgence.

This tenuous but quiet peace persisted until [[History|The Age of Heroes]] when the disgrace of two of Mélarrne's great houses shook the city's long-standing stability. The Delameur and Therizon families were found to be in violation of the terms of Mélarrne's pact with the empire, dealing in heretical practices that resulted in the proliferation of the undead. Delameur fled the city he so long held dear, while the Therizon family were imprisoned locally in Azkhar. Both houses' holdings were seized by the Empire and their management distributed among the other great houses. The resulting power vacuum created opportunists among the more ambitious members of the Grand Council, and led to an age of uncertainty for the otherwise thriving city-state. 
### Politics
The systems of Mélarrne's republic appear esoteric to those not born into its political sphere. Many locals even find it difficult to navigate its intricacies. It is a process which is guided as much by chance as it is by rationality. This unique blend of fate and democracy have contributed to Mélarrne's stability over its centuries-long journey. It is commonly boasted in Mélarrne that one won't find a conflict of succession within its borders.

The highest station in the city-state of Mélarrne is called the *Héldrion*, a noble house which is given the final say in matters of legislation, finances, and foreign relations. Every ten years the Héldrion is selected via a highly-public ritual called the **Ninefold Veil** performed over nine days by the city's Grand Council, which is itself made up of dozens of the city's wealthiest merchants and guildsmen. Its stages are as follows:
1.  **The Lantern Draw.** Each interested member of the Grand Council sends a mask by courier to the Hall or Waters. A seer-child called the *Kharthas* is robed in lotus silks before drawing twenty-seven masks from a basin filled with black water and floating pearls. These twenty-seven are named the Dawnbearers.
2. **The Petal Cull.** The twenty-seven Dawnbearers gather around a pool in the Garden Hall, where priests have scattered lotus petals. Nine of these are painted with red runes on their bottom face. Those who are seated where the runed petals drift are selected to become the nine Nightbearers.
3. **The Night Winds.** The nine Nightbearers are led into a room lit only by the embers of incense. Each whispers names which are voted upon by the other Nightbearers. The process continues until forty-five names receive seven out of nine votes to be named the Forty-Five Winds.
4. **The Salt Purge.** Each of the Forty-Five Winds are given pearls, eighteen of which dissolve when submersed in brine. The pearls can be traded once before being cast into a ewer of brine. The eighteen whose pearls dissolve are named the Salt's Chosen.
5. **The Oar Tide.** Each of the Salt's chosen is given three ivory rods onto which are painted the names of nominees. These fifty-four rods are affixed to eighteen gondolas by masked shipwrights.
6. **The Garland March.** The eighteen gondolas are driven through the city's canals during an evening festival. The public throws garlands purchased at the festival, attempting to ring the ivory rod of their favoured candidate. The nine with the most garlands are selected as the Perfumed Nine.
7. **The Flame Fingers.** Each of the Perfumed Nine is given five lanterns onto which are branded the seals of their favoured nominees. The garlands are crushed and distributed between the five lanterns and lit at midnight. The twenty-seven seals whose lanterns remain alight by morning are named the Flame Consorts.
8. **The Ember Breath.** The twenty-seven Flame Consorts don ceremonial masks and breathe incense until faintness. In their trance, each speaks two names that must receive a majority of votes to be added to a ledger of electorates. Those admitted become the Fifty-Four Masks.
9. **The Saints' Resolve.** The Fifty-Four Masks don concealing robes and veils before entering the Hall of Drowned Saints. There in the presence of the bodies of a thousand drowned priests lies a copy of the ledger carved into a great wooden tablet. The Masks vote in secret, placing white stones for yea and black stones for nay by the names they pass. The first name to collect twenty-five white stones is named Mélarrne's Héldrion. They don Serenity's Veil and do not appear again to appear unveiled in public view. 
### Climate
Mélarrne endures a tropical monsoon climate, marked by sweltering heat, heavy rains, and oppressive humidity. The long wet season turns the Arjos Wetlands into a floodplain, swelling canals and washing detritus from the jungle into the city’s waterways. Sudden storms sweep in from the sea, lashing the cliffs with lightning and rain before dispersing into blue skies.

The dry season brings relief but not coolness—air grows heavy, streets shimmer in heat, and water becomes brackish in the farther canals. Locals adapt with layered silks, wide-brimmed hats, and a cuisine rich in spice and sourness to balance the stifling weather. Foreign merchants will complain, but Mélarrne’s citizens consider it a part of the region's natural rhythm; a part of life no different than trade or tide.
### Culture
Mélarrne is a city of veils and lanterns, of ritual spectacle and mercantile splendour. Born in exile and raised on trade, its cosmopolitan spirit has remained strong through years of independence and through assimilation into the greater Mirian Empire. Though the city has tamed the nearby jungle, the survivalist cunning of old resonates through the hearts and minds of all Mélarrne's peoples.

**Public Life.** Public behaviour in Mélarrne is shaped by a strong emphasis on presentation. In the home or during festivals, hospitality and warmth are expected, but in commerce or politics individuals adopt a much more shrewd and cold demeanour. The use of veils or masks in professional contexts is common, both as a symbol of discretion and to serve the purpose of obscuring one's facial cues. Public social interaction tends toward a formal and measured etiquette that often contrasts with the city's tropical climate, and favours composure over displays of emotion.

**Artistry & Luxury.** Mélarrne's arts emphasise luxury and spectacle that reflects the city-state's mercantile worth and diverse cultural heritage. Performance traditions such as theatre, dance, and processions are central to civic life. Veil-craft and mask-making are highly-regarded crafts, with workshops producing lacquered, gilded and inlaid designs that serve commercial and ceremonial purposes. Decorative arts such as silk-weaving, ivory carving, and lacquer work are significant exports, while public patronage ensures the presence of statues, frescoes, and mosaics throughout the city.

**Architecture.** Architecture in Mélarrne is distinguished by the draconic influence of the Fellreach myths, visible in the ornamentation of its major structures. Rooftops are sharply pointed, their back-bowed ridges decorated with fang-like spikes that recall the image of a dragon's spine and serve the purpose of warding away birds and climbing pests. Public buildings features sweeping eaves and elaborate lacquered cornices shaped to resemble wings or scales, while private homes in wealthier districts display dragon-headed water spouts and carved lintels. This aesthetic projects the symbolism of dragons as the thoughtful guardians of wealth and power.

**Fashion.** Mélarni fashion is light and breezy, along with being largely gender-neutral. Flowing garments are layered in ways that optimise ventilation, with veils and sashes added to achieve a desirable triangular silhouette. Masks are often worn by merchants and public officials during business hours and on formal occasions. Shoes are famously tall, sandals and slippers which are elevated to keep wearers above the damp ground. Hair is kept long and elaborately-styled, often pinned with combs or ornamented with fresh flowers. Colours vary wildly, but are largely vibrant, with whites and golds being used to project wealth or status.

**Cuisine.** The Mélarni diet is widely varied and highly tinged with spice. Staples such as fish, shellfish, rice, and tropical fruits are augmented by delicacies extracted from the nearby Fellreach. Signature dishes include tamarind-glazed centipede, chili-lime soups, candied lizard tails, and even the tough roasted flesh of jungle drakes which is eaten ceremonially. Street foods such as skewered meats, fried dumplings, and pickled vegetables are commonly eaten in small portions between larger meals that are served banquet-style in order to enforce a sense of community. 

**Faith.** Mélarni faith is pluralistic, with gods, demons, and spirits being regarded as beneficial or malignant actors present on the same stage as earthly denizens. Of the many willspawn honoured in Mélarrne, [[Dwir]], [[Nali]], and [[Khata]] enjoyed the largest congregations prior to the adoption of [[Miria|Mirian]] worship in the late Age of Despair. Worship is often transactional rather than devotional, with offerings of flowers, food, and coin being given in exchange for blessings. Similarly, the role of priests is more that of mediation and the keeping of ritual rather than serving as a moral authority. 

**Marital Rites.** Marriages in Mélarrne are typically arranged to some degree, balancing personal compatibility with mercantile or political advantage. Love-matches and informal partnerships are not uncommon, but are rarely given the same ceremony as their formal counterparts. Wedding ceremonies are lengthy, veiled affairs. Marriage contracts are read aloud before witnesses prior to the rituals of ancestor blessing, the exchange of tokens, and public procession along the canals. Feasts follow the formal ceremonies, with the more prominent families opening receptions to the public as a show of wealth and status. Guild oversite pervades any of these traditions to ensure that trade alliances remain recognised and binding.

**Funerary Practices.** Originally, Mirian burial traditions were honoured in Mélarrne, but following the unearthing of corpses and the resulting Narthonic Plague of 504 AR, burial is now strictly forbidden within the city's limits. At that time, House Cavareth presented the alternative of stripping and burning the infectious flesh from the dead, leaving behind only clean, white bone. Over time, these came to be objects of ornamental display in homes, and later, in open mausoleums dotted around public parks. The city's most prominent dead are displayed in the *Maergha Thartha* or Floating Mausoleum that drifts in the [[Zhiago Bay]]. The funerals themselves are typically light affairs following a wake and keening. Much like weddings, they are appended by feasts and performance to honour the life of the deceased. In Mélarrne, death is as much a thing of mourning as it is a celebration, of life, love, and peace eternal.
### Population
Mélarrne is a bustling city with over 170,000 citizens per the most recent census. Few can trace their lineage back to the original refugees who founded the city during the Empire's Golden Age, as centuries of trade and migration have led the city to become markedly cosmopolitan. Nearly every ancestry can find representation along its canals. The city-state measures citizenship in contracts rather than bloodlines, and foreign traders often settle permanently in order to secure their family's mercantile legacy.
### Economy
The Mélarni economy is defined by its strategic geography and long tradition of mercantile enterprise. Its position provides access to routes along the Shiozsho River, routes along the Kalakalan Coast, and routes and across the Gulf of Chyrrhos. Mélarrne largely trades in consumable exotic luxuries such as chilies, tamarind, sugarcane, and rare fruits that are processed into stable items that are transferrable across distances. Artisan works are also highly desired commodities in foreign courts. Among the most expensive of Mélarni exports are those extracted from the Fellreach. Drake scales, gorgon's eyes, and Ilsyra inks are among the more rare products sought after by foreign markets. Though highly prevalent, trade is heavily monitored by guilds and houses chartered by the Mélarni Republic, each of which is responsible for maintaining their fleets and warehouses to an appreciable standards. The civic treasury is funded largely by tariffs on imports and the licensure of river and sea trade routes, creating a balance between public funds and private wealth. Through these channels, Mélarrne has secured its reputation as a city of luxury and rarity, a nexus where one can find the most exotic items.
